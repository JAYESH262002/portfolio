07 / FIELD NOTESOBSERVATIONS FROM THE COUNTER AND THE CUSTOMS PORT

Frontline lessons

Notes on how things really move.

Working principles formed on retail beat walks in Mumbai and international sourcing calls.

NOTE 01

The Phantom Stockout

When a distributor reports adequate stock on paper, but retail shelves run empty on Saturday evening, the issue isn't demand forecasting—it's delivery cycle synchronization. The delivery van's dispatch window must match the retailer's cash collection rhythm.

CAVINKARE FMCG BEAT
NOTE 02

The Landed-Cost Trap

The cheapest overseas factory quote is often the most expensive inventory you will ever buy. Once freight volatility, customs tariff inspection delays, and working-capital carrying costs are added, regional suppliers with higher initial price tags often win on net margin.

ONLY SOLAR SOURCING
NOTE 03

SOPs vs Tuesday Mornings

A process that requires five manual clicks will be abandoned on a busy Tuesday morning. Good operational design eliminates friction at the frontline, creating default paths that make doing the right thing the easiest thing.
